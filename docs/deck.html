Solution

Four moves, all on-chain.

1

Issuer creates the invoice

Amount, due date, and late-fee terms (e.g. 0.5%/day, capped at 10%) live in a PDA. No signup, no accounts.

2

Payer signs once

One signature approves the program’s vault PDA as an SPL delegate: “pull amount + max fee on the due date.”

3

Collection is permissionless

At due, anyone can trigger it — the issuer, a bot, KSeF middleware. The program signs as delegate and the fee is computed on-chain.

4

Optional advance (LP)

An investor pays the issuer today at a discount; at collection the waterfall is payer → LP → issuer. Positions are priced, not promised.

Like a standing order — but for invoices, with teeth.

How it works

7 instructions. One PDA per invoice.

issuer—create→invoice PDA←authorize—payer (SPL delegate = vault PDA)
due date reached · anyone may collect
payer—amount + fee→issuer│ no LP
payer—payout→LPandissuer (total − payout)│ advanced
late_fee = floor(amount × fee_bps_per_day × full_days_late / 10 000) · capped at fee_cap_bps
enforced by the program — not a promise · transfers via transfer_checked signed by the vault_authority PDA

Why this works on Solana

Cheap enough for real invoices.

economics

Fractional-cent fees make a 5,000 tUSD invoice economically viable — unlike L1 smart contracts.

primitive

The SPL token delegate is the native “pull payment” primitive — a mandate is one signature.

permissionless

Collection can be triggered by anyone: the issuer, a cron bot, or KSeF e-invoicing middleware.

disputes

Fee math lives on-chain — no “the reminder never arrived,” no arguments about interest.
